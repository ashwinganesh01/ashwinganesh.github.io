About Venus

Venus is the second planet from the Sun and is often called Earth's “sister planet” because both planets are similar in size and structure.

Despite these similarities, Venus is extremely dangerous. Its atmosphere is filled with thick carbon dioxide clouds that trap heat through a greenhouse effect.

Surface temperatures on Venus can reach around 465°C, making it the hottest planet in the Solar System — even hotter than Mercury.

Venus also rotates differently from most planets. It spins backwards, meaning the Sun rises in the west and sets in the east.

  • Distance from Sun: 108.2 million km
  • Length of day: 243 Earth days
  • Length of year: 225 Earth days
  • Surface temperature: ~465°C
  • Number of moons: 0

The Surface of Venus

Venus has a rocky surface covered with mountains, volcanoes, and huge lava plains.

Scientists believe that thousands of volcanoes exist on Venus, and some may still be active today.

The pressure on Venus is extremely strong — nearly 90 times greater than the pressure on Earth.

  • Atmosphere mainly made of carbon dioxide
  • Clouds contain sulfuric acid
  • Strong greenhouse effect traps heat
  • No liquid water exists on the surface

Space Missions to Venus

Venera Missions

The Soviet Union's Venera spacecraft were the first missions to successfully land on Venus and send images back to Earth.

Magellan Mission

NASA's Magellan spacecraft used radar mapping to study the surface of Venus and reveal its volcanic landscape.

Future Exploration

Scientists continue studying Venus to understand climate change, volcanic activity, and the possibility of past oceans.
